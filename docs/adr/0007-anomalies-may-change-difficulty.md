# Weekly anomalies may change difficulty; the thing to protect is the floor, not the ceiling

The anomaly framework (issue #141) shipped with a comparability firewall: every `AnomalyDef` carried a `distorting` flag, measured by `scripts/anomaly-guardrail.ts` against a maxed tier-3 board, and any anomaly that moved the depth ceiling more than about ±2 waves was held back until a per-week leaderboard partition existed. That partition was never built, so in practice the rule was a ship gate. It shelved The Long Dark, Sudden Death, Archetype Lock, and eventually Teeming Dark, and it is the reason the three anomalies that did ship were pure composition reshuffles — the only shapes that could clear the bar. All three were deleted on 2026-08-08 for being too small to notice, which is the gate working exactly as designed and producing exactly the wrong outcome.

The firewall existed to protect max-depth comparability on a global, cross-week leaderboard. That frame no longer carries the game. Depth is saturated — every 45-clear across three seasons came from roughly three tier-3 units, and 6 of 6 real riders maxed the 2026-07-27 season by day 3 (#163) — and the nightly PvP league (#154/#157) is now the scoring metric, with PvE serving primarily as a funding track. PvP is *relative* scoring settled between players inside the same week, so a week-wide difficulty change is symmetric: every board is equally affected and the duel outcome is untouched. Relative scoring self-normalizes against a moved difficulty baseline in a way absolute scoring never can. We are therefore retiring the ±2-wave ship gate. `distorting` survives as measured information — useful for labelling a week, and for partitioning depth scores if that board is ever restored (#172) — but it no longer blocks an anomaly from shipping.

What replaces it are two guardrails that speak to the game we actually have. First, **measure the floor, not the ceiling**: the question is no longer "does a maxed board's ceiling move" but "is the week still playable for a realistic player," which means the `balance:realistic` band rather than the maxed-board fixture. An anomaly that halves a normal player's depth is not unfair, but it may be miserable, and misery is now the failure mode worth gating on. Second, **watch the income coupling**: income is depth-proportional (ADR-0002), so a harder week silently shrinks every player's bank and therefore the PvP board they can field. That is symmetric and so still fair, but past some depth it degrades the league itself into a tier-1 chaff fight. `anomaly-guardrail.ts` now reports implied weekly income alongside depth for this reason. Two things are explicitly *not* relaxed: ADR-0003's compounding law is absolute and unaffected, and forcing an enemy archetype stays rejected — its difficulty objection is moot under this ADR, but its independent variance objection ("this week is armored" plays the same every time it comes up) survives the 2026-08-08 cull intact.
